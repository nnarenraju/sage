# ELI5 — Science Explained from Scratch

These notebooks take you on a journey from **how sound works** all the way to **how
scientists use machine learning to hunt for gravitational waves from colliding black holes**
— and where that machine learning goes wrong.

You do not need to know anything going in. No physics, no maths, no coding experience.
Just curiosity and a willingness to press a button and see what happens.

---

## How to Use These Notebooks

**Go in order, one notebook at a time.** Each one builds on the last.

1. Open the first notebook: `00_frequencies.ipynb`
2. In the menu bar, click **Run All** (or press **Shift + Enter** on each cell from top to bottom)
3. Read, listen, and look at the pictures — in that order
4. If something feels confusing, keep going. It almost always clicks one notebook later
5. Experiment! Change a number in any code cell and press **Shift + Enter** to re-run it.
   That is literally how scientists work.

> **Use headphones.** Several notebooks have audio that really matters. The difference
> between hearing the signal appear is what makes this click.

## The Full Journey

By the time you reach the last notebook in this series, you will understand:

- What a **gravitational wave** is and how it travels across the universe

## Tips for Getting the Most Out of This

🎧 **Put headphones in.** You will listen to things disappearing into noise and reappearing
after a simple transformation. That moment of hearing the signal come back is unforgettable.

🔁 **If a cell looks broken, restart and run all.** Go to the menu: *Kernel → Restart & Run All*.

🧪 **Break things on purpose.** Change a frequency, a duration, a noise level — run the cell
again and see what changes. You learn far more from breaking things than from reading about them.

📸 **Look before you read the caption.** Every plot tells a story. Try to figure out what you
are seeing before reading the title. Your instincts will surprise you.

💬 **There are no stupid questions.** If something does not make sense, write it down and ask.
The concepts in these notebooks took physicists decades to work out — you are not expected to
get everything instantly.
